The Key Discovery: Sigmoid Scaling

The researchers discovered something crucial: RL training follows a predictable S-shaped (sigmoid) curve. This means you can mathematically predict how good your model will become based on how much compute you use!

R(C) = A / (1 + (C_mid / C)^B)
Translation: Performance R depends on compute C, following a predictable curve defined by just 3 parameters

The Three Magic Parameters

A
Asymptotic Performance
(The ceiling)
B
Compute Efficiency
(How fast you improve)
C_mid
Midpoint
(When you're halfway there)
lightbulb Like Learning to Play Guitar

When you learn guitar, you improve quickly at first (strumming chords), then progress slows as you tackle harder techniques. Eventually, you hit a ceiling based on your practice approach. The sigmoid curve captures this exact pattern - rapid early gains, then diminishing returns as you approach your limit.

stars Key Insight

By fitting just a few early data points to this curve, researchers can predict the final performance of a massive training run before spending all that compute. This is like knowing how good a chef will be after years of practice by watching them cook for just a few hours!

The ScaleRL Recipe: 7 Key Ingredients

After exhaustive experimentation, the team identified the essential ingredients for successful large-scale RL training:

1

Use CISPO Loss Function

A special "truncated importance sampling" loss that prevents training from going haywire. It clips extreme values to keep learning stable.

2

PipelineRL Architecture

Run generation and training asynchronously in a pipeline. While the model generates new responses, another copy is training on previous ones.

3

FP32 for the LM Head

Use full 32-bit precision for the language model's output layer. This tiny change prevents numerical instability at large scale.

4

High Replay Ratio

Reuse generated data efficiently - train on each piece of data multiple times (up to 50x) before generating new samples.

5

Longer Generation Lengths

Let the model "think" longer during training (8K-32K tokens). Longer reasoning chains lead to better final performance.

6

On-Policy Training

Train on data generated by your current model, not old data from previous versions. Freshness matters for RL!

7

Gradient Accumulation

Accumulate gradients across many batches before updating, creating effectively larger batch sizes for more stable learning.

The Result: Following this recipe, the team successfully completed a 100,000 GPU-hour training run - equivalent to running a single GPU continuously for over 11 years, but done in parallel over days!

The Four Axes of Scaling

When scaling up RL training, you can turn four different "knobs". The paper reveals which ones matter most:

memory

Model Scale

Bigger models = better performance ceiling, but same efficiency

text_fields

Generation Length

Longer "thinking" = higher ceiling AND better efficiency

view_module

Batch Size

More samples = faster training per step

timer

Training Steps

More iterations = closer to the ceiling

stars Surprising Finding

Generation length is the most efficient axis! Letting the model think longer during training (8K-32K tokens) both raises the performance ceiling AND improves compute efficiency. It's like getting more bang for your buck.

What About Problem Difficulty?

The team tested on problems of varying difficulty (using AIME math competition questions rated 1-5). They found that while the overall performance ceiling varies, the scaling behavior is consistent. Harder problems just shift the curves - the S-shape remains!

Leave-One-Out Analysis

The team tested what happens when you remove each ingredient from the recipe:

Most Critical Components:
  • CISPO loss: Without it, training becomes unstable and can collapse
  • FP32 precision: Using lower precision causes numerical issues at scale
  • High replay ratio: Low replay wastes compute on excessive data generation

Glossary: Key Terms Explained

Reinforcement Learning (RL) - A training approach where models learn by getting rewards for good outputs. Like training a dog with treats!
Sigmoid Curve - An S-shaped curve that starts slow, accelerates in the middle, then levels off. Describes many natural learning processes.
GPU-Hours - A measure of compute: one GPU running for one hour. 100K GPU-hours = 100,000 GPUs for 1 hour, or 1 GPU for 11+ years.
CISPO - Clipped Importance Sampling Policy Optimization. A fancy way of keeping RL training stable by limiting extreme updates.
PipelineRL - Running data generation and model training at the same time (asynchronously) to maximize GPU efficiency.
Replay Ratio - How many times you train on each piece of generated data. Higher = more efficient use of generated samples.
FP32/FP16 - Number precision formats. FP32 uses 32 bits (more precise), FP16 uses 16 bits (faster but less precise).
On-Policy vs Off-Policy - On-policy trains on data from the current model; off-policy uses older data. On-policy is fresher but more expensive.
AIME - American Invitational Mathematics Examination. A hard math competition used to test AI reasoning ability.
Asymptotic Performance - The theoretical maximum performance you'd reach with infinite compute. The "ceiling" of what's achievable.

Key Takeaways

  1. RL scaling is predictable - It follows sigmoid curves that can be characterized with just 3 parameters.
  2. The ScaleRL recipe works - 7 key ingredients enable reliable training at 100K+ GPU-hour scale.
  3. Generation length matters most - Letting models "think longer" is the most efficient way to improve.
  4. Early prediction is possible - A few data points can forecast the entire training trajectory.
  5. Details matter at scale - Small choices (like FP32 precision) become critical at large scale.
