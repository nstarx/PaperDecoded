error_outline The Problem: Siloed Fraud Detection

Every bank has a unique view of the financial world. A bank focused on Asia sees different fraud patterns than one serving North America. But here's the catch: they can't share their data due to privacy laws, regulations, and competitive concerns.

cancel Without Collaboration

  • Each bank only knows its own fraud patterns
  • New fraud types go undetected
  • Cross-border attacks slip through
  • Siloed data = siloed protection

check_circle With Federated Learning

  • Banks share knowledge, not data
  • Global fraud coverage
  • Privacy fully preserved
  • Collective intelligence

share What is Federated Learning?

Federated Learning (FL) is a revolutionary approach where multiple organizations train a shared AI model without ever exchanging their raw data. Instead, they only share the model's learned parameters - its "weights."

psychology The Core Concept

Each participant trains locally on their own data, producing a model that captures their unique insights. These local models are then aggregated into a global model that benefits from everyone's knowledge - without anyone seeing anyone else's data.

The Federated Learning Process

1. Local Training

Each bank trains an AI model on their own transaction data, learning their local fraud patterns

2. Share Weights Only

Banks send their model weights (numerical parameters) to a central aggregator - no raw data ever leaves

3. Aggregate

The central server combines all weights using algorithms like FedAvg (Federated Averaging)

4. Distribute Global Model

The improved model is sent back to all participants, now smarter than any individual model

5. Repeat

Multiple rounds of this process lead to a converged, highly capable global model

lightbulb The Chef Analogy

Imagine 10 chefs each perfecting their own pasta sauce recipe. Instead of sharing their secret ingredients, they share only cooking tips: "cook longer," "add more salt." A master coordinator combines all the tips into ultimate cooking guidelines. Each chef keeps their secret recipe, but everyone makes better sauce!

What Are "Model Weights"?

Model weights are the numerical values that an AI model learns during training. Think of them as the model's "knowledge" encoded as numbers. A neural network might have millions of weights that together determine how it makes predictions. Sharing these weights doesn't reveal the original data - it only shares the patterns learned from that data.

science The Experiment Setup

The project used synthetic data to simulate real-world financial transactions with two types of anomalies. Here's how it was structured:

25K Training Rows/Bank

Anomaly Types Tested

location_on Location Anomalies

When a user's transaction location differs significantly from their expected location. Example: Someone who usually transacts in London suddenly makes a payment from a cash counter in Lagos.

schedule Account Age Anomalies

When a new account makes disproportionately large transactions. Example: An account that's only 2 days old moving $20 million - normal accounts need 12+ weeks to build such limits.

The Neural Network

Architecture: A Deep Neural Network with 2 hidden layers (16 and 8 neurons)

  • Learning Rate: 0.001 (reduced from 0.01 to prevent oscillation)
  • Epochs: 5 per training round
  • Batch Size: 32
  • Framework: Keras with Flower for federated coordination

Model Explainability

The team used SHAP values (SHapley Additive exPlanations) to understand which features drove the model's decisions. This helps ensure the AI isn't making predictions based on spurious correlations.

lightbulb What are SHAP Values?

Imagine an AI predicts a transaction is fraudulent. SHAP tells you why: "The location distance contributed +0.6 to the fraud score, account age contributed +0.3, and transaction amount contributed +0.1." It's like having the AI show its work!

What Happened at Each Stage

Round 0 (Before Aggregation)

Each bank's model excels at its specialty but fails completely on the other type. Bank 1 catches location fraud but misses account-age fraud entirely (and vice versa).

Round 1 (First Aggregation)

Temporary dip! Performance drops for both. Why? The weights shifted but haven't fully integrated the new knowledge yet. This is normal and expected.

Rounds 2-3 (Recovery)

With local retraining after each aggregation, models start recovering. They're learning to detect both anomaly types while retaining their original strengths.

Round 4 (Convergence)

Success! Both models achieve high precision and recall on BOTH anomaly types. The global model performs as well as if it had been trained on all data combined.

menu_book Glossary

Federated Learning (FL) - A machine learning approach where multiple parties train models collaboratively without sharing their raw data, only model updates.
FedAvg (Federated Averaging) - The most common algorithm for aggregating model weights from multiple participants by computing a weighted average.
Model Weights - The numerical parameters that a neural network learns during training, encoding its knowledge and decision-making logic.
Aggregation Round - One complete cycle of local training followed by centralized weight aggregation and redistribution.
SHAP Values - A technique from game theory that explains which input features contributed most to a model's prediction.
Non-IID Data - Non-Independently and Identically Distributed data, meaning each participant's data follows different patterns (the real-world scenario).
Privacy-Enhancing Technologies (PETs) - Techniques like differential privacy, homomorphic encryption, and secure multiparty computation that add extra security layers.
Kinexys - J.P. Morgan's blockchain and digital payments platform that developed this proof-of-concept.
AIKYA - Sanskrit word meaning "unity" or "oneness," reflecting the collaborative nature of the project.
